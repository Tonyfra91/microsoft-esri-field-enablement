## The business question

> What information is missing before we compare possible service sites?

This is a hypothetical discovery example, not a location recommendation,
investment model, customer reference, or validated site-selection solution.

## Data and people

Begin with [original synthetic points](../04-demos/data/README.md). Their
fictional request counts do not represent demand, population, or capacity.
No candidate sites or service boundaries are present in that file.

A later private evaluation would need approved candidate locations and relevant
constraints, such as access, population, capacity, and applicable planning rules.
The business owner and GIS analyst must decide which evidence is appropriate
and lawful to use. Include the data owner and relevant local specialists.

## Choose and demonstrate a first step

1. Use the [Excel conversation card](../02-integration-catalog/microsoft-365/excel.md)
   and [draft five-minute demo](../04-demos/excel-five-minute-demo.md) to introduce
   geographic context without ranking sites.
2. Ask the audience to identify what the point map cannot answer.
3. If the question requires geographic analysis across large Fabric datasets,
   explore [GeoAnalytics](../02-integration-catalog/fabric/geoanalytics.md)
   with the data platform owner. This is a separate qualification, not an
   automatically supported extension of the Excel demo.
4. Use the [comparison guide](../02-integration-catalog/compare-integrations.md)
   and [technical checklist](../05-architecture/technical-qualification.md)
   to choose a small, evidence-backed evaluation.

## Proposed evaluation criteria

| Test | Proposed acceptance criterion | Responsible role |
|------|-------------------------------|------------------|
| Basic interpretation | The user distinguishes known locations from unknown service coverage | Business owner |
| Missing information | The team records at least three required additional inputs and who can supply them | GIS analyst and data owner |
| Analysis definition | One question has agreed inputs, method, expected output, and limitations before analysis begins | GIS analyst |
| Decision boundaries | The team explicitly agrees that the sample cannot rank or recommend sites | Evaluation owner |

Do not convert fictional counts into an ROI or suitability score.
Pause if the analysis depends on unavailable data, unsupported capabilities,
or unresolved regional and privacy requirements.

## Agree on the next action

The business owner confirms the decision. The GIS analyst proposes the method
and source evidence; the data owner checks reuse rights and quality.
Record a review date, success criteria, and cleanup steps in the
[evaluation plan](../06-sales-enablement/meeting-templates.md#evaluation-plan).
