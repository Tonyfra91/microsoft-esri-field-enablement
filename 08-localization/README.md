## Localization

English is the initial working language. No translations are included yet.

## Write for an international audience

Use concrete words that explain the action before someone follows a link.
Keep product names unchanged and explain specialized terms when first needed.
Avoid idioms, unexplained abbreviations, and phrases that only make sense to a
particular sales organization.

| Avoid | Prefer |
|-------|--------|
| Which catalog candidate merits investigation? | Which Microsoft and Esri integration should we explore? |
| Who consumes the data? | Who uses the data? |
| What freshness is required? | How often must the data update? |
| Who owns the outcome? | Who is responsible for the decision? |
| A technical deep dive | A detailed technical discussion |

Use ISO dates, such as 2026-10-30, and include time zones in meeting plans.
Confirm CSV delimiters, decimal separators, coordinate order, units, and regional
settings before presenting a demo. Do not silently change numbers when translating.
Accessibility includes meaningful link text and image descriptions, not only language.

## Translate with a local reviewer

For a future translation:

* Identify the source page, language, reviewer, and source review date
* Preserve product names, official links, caveats, and validation status
* Distinguish translated field guidance from official localized documentation
* Do not infer regional availability, licensing, or compliance from a translation
* Link the translated page back to its source and flag outdated content

Translation requests and reviews can follow [CONTRIBUTING](../CONTRIBUTING.md).
Use only public information when describing regional needs.
