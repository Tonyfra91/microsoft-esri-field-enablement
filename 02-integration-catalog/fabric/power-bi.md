## In one sentence

ArcGIS for Power BI adds mapping capabilities to Power BI reports and dashboards.

## Why a customer cares

* See how a business measure varies across places
* Compare report data with geographic context from ArcGIS
* Explore location as part of an existing reporting workflow

These are evaluation opportunities, not promised business outcomes.

## Listen for this

> "We have Power BI everywhere."

> "We want to understand service coverage, not only total demand."

## Questions to ask

* Which report and decision would benefit from a geographic view?
* What location fields and authoritative geographic data are available?
* Who creates the report, and who needs to view or share it?
* Which account, tenant, privacy, and licensing requirements apply?

## See it

[Microsoft: Create an ArcGIS map in Power BI](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/visuals/power-bi-visualizations-arcgis)
is an official tutorial, not a repository-owned demo or a government reference
implementation.

Consider this candidate when a customer wants geographic context within a
Power BI reporting workflow. Confirm the audience, datasets, and intended
decision before selecting an evaluation.
